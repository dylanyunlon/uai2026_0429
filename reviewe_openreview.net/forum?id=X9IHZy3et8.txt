</div></div><div><strong class="note-content-field disable-tex-rendering">Q4 weaknesses:</strong> <div class="note-content-value markdown-rendered"><p>(1) The proposed framework may be overly artificial relative to current scientific LLM practice. The paper introduces a fairly elaborate pipeline built around fact-option decomposition, approximate deducibility graphs, progressive validation, and dynamic branching. While this is conceptually interesting, it is not fully clear that such a structured intervention is necessary in light of recent agentic LLM systems, which already exhibit strong self-verification and tool-using behavior on scientific reasoning tasks without requiring such handcrafted intermediate structures. As a result, the practical relevance of the proposed framework to the current frontier of scientific LLM development is somewhat unclear.
(2) The empirical gains appear modest relative to the complexity of the method. Although the paper reports improvements on several benchmarks, the raw benchmark gains are not especially large—for instance, the main PhyX accuracy gain is limited relative to the complexity and computational overhead of the full pipeline. Much of the strongest empirical improvement is in the paper’s own validity-oriented metrics rather than in standard benchmark accuracy, which weakens the overall case that the method offers a compelling practical advance
(3) The experimental evaluation is not fully convincing for current LLMs. The empirical setup mixes multiple models across different parts of the pipeline, and some of the comparisons appear to involve older or heterogeneous systems rather than a clean evaluation against current frontier scientific or agentic LLMs. This makes it difficult to isolate the contribution of the proposed conformal-control framework itself, and it leaves open the possibility that the method is less competitive than modern tool-augmented or self-validating agentic approaches on contemporary science benchmarks.</p>

<li><p>The ACF hierarchy (Definitions 3-5) is a real conceptual contribution. Separating independent factuality, coherent factuality, and absolute-coherent-factuality into three levels with a strict hierarchy (Proposition 6) is clean and well-motivated. Prefix closure (Observation 1) and cascade prevention (Lemma 7) follow naturally and capture something real about error propagation in scientific reasoning chains.</p>
</li>
<li><p>The paper builds on Mohri &amp; Hashimoto (2024) and Rubin-Toles et al. (2025) in a way that feels like a natural progression. The transition from independence-based filtering to coherent factuality to ACF is logically clear and the paper explains where prior approaches fall short for scientific domains.</p>
</li>
<li><p>The ablation in Table 1 is informative, showing how progressive+scientific validation outperforms individual components. The equal-budget comparison against best-of-N and rejection sampling in Table 9 is especially useful, since it rules out the explanation that improvements just come from spending more compute. This goes beyond what many papers do.</p>
</li>
</ol>
</div></div><div><strong class="note-content-field disable-tex-rendering">Q4 weaknesses:</strong> <div class="note-content-value markdown-rendered"><ol>
<li><p>The paper's baseline numbers have serious issues. The introduction claims GPT-4 gets "43.5%" on PhyX but the experiments section reports "45.8%" as the GPT-4 baseline. These can't both be correct for the same model on the same benchmark, and neither number matches publicly available PhyX results (which list GPT-4o at roughly 32.5%). Without clarity on which exact model variant was evaluated under what conditions, the claimed margin of SFC (50.1%) over "GPT-4" is hard to interpret.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Algorithm 2 and Algorithm 3 are identical, both titled "Scientific Sentence Validation" with the same pseudocode. Section 5.3 describes Algorithm 3 as the graph-structured conformal prediction procedure for candidate subgraph generation, which should be an entirely different algorithm. This is a copy-paste error, meaning the core algorithmic contribution (graph-structured CP) is missing from the paper.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Doubao is used as the base model but not well justified or contextualized. There is no standalone evaluation of Doubao's baseline performance on these benchmarks, so it's unclear how much of the improvement comes from SFC vs the base model being stronger than expected. PhyX is multimodal but the paper doesn't clarify whether evaluation is text-only or image+text, or whether Doubao supports vision inputs at all.</p>
</li>
<li><p>The "scientific ground truth" T (Definition 2) is operationally vague. The paper never specifies what T concretely is in the experiments. A "physics-trained language model" is used for fact-option splitting, but if the ground truth itself depends on an LLM, the conformal guarantees rest on the LLM's correctness, creating a circularity not adequtely addressed.</p>
</li>
</ol>
</div></div><div><strong class="note-content-field disable-tex-rendering">Q5 comments:</strong> <div class="note-content-value markdown-rendered"><p>Some more specific points and suggestions:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Eq (25) defines the graph-level nonconformity score r(X, Y, G) and requires checking whether V_tau "is ACF." Per Definition 5 this involves checking deducibility, but the paper never gives a concrete implementation of this check for the nonconformity score computation. Is ACF just operationalized as the composite score sigma not exceeding tau? If so, it should be stated explicitly.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Theorem 14 claims conformal coverage for dynamic graphs. The proof relies on G(X,Y) being deterministic, but Section 6 reports only "94% consistency across equivalent inputs" for the template-based graph construction. The 6% inconsistency means G is not deterministic in practice, which could violate Theorem 14's premise. This gap should be discussed honestly.</p>
</li>
<li><p>The conformal guarantee (Eq 26) requires exchangeability, but domain-specific calibration (Section 5.5) splits the data into per-domain sets. With the total calibration set being only 50 examples (Appendix I), domain-specific subsets might be very small. The paper should report domain-specific calibration set sizes.</p>
</li>
<li><p>In Table 1, the "Progressive + Graph" config achieves 52.4% accuracy on PhyX, higher than full SFC at 50.1%. The paper notes "potential over-validation" but doesn't investigate when or why adding scientific validation hurts accuracy. Is the 4pp validity gain (87.6 to 91.7) worth the 2.3pp accuracy loss? This deserves more analysis.</p>
</li>
<li><p>The 73% reduction in scientific law violations is claimed in the abstract and Section 6 but I couldn't find a clear definition of what counts as a violation or how this percentage was computed. On which dataset? Compared to what baseline output?</p>
</li>
<li><p>The inter-annotator agreement of kappa=0.82 is reported but the paper omits how many annotators there were, how many examples they evaluated, and how disagreements were resolved.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Mohri &amp; Hashimoto (2024) appeared at ICML 2024 (PMLR vol 235) but is cited as an arXiv preprint. Should cite the published version.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Several directly relevant papers are missing: Quach et al. "Conformal Language Modeling" (ICLR 2024) and Cherian, Gibbs &amp; Candès "Large Language Model Validity via Enhanced Conformal Prediction Methods" (NeurIPS 2024). Both are closely related to the confromal prediction + LLM factuality space.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Table 2 reports "Overall" PhyX validity as 91.2% but the abstract and Table 1 report 91.7%. These numbers should be consistent if they both refer to PhyX.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Section 5.5 contains an incomplete sentence: "We address exhibit distinct error" which appears garbled.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Questions I'd like addressed in rebuttal:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which exact GPT model variant was used as baseline? Can you reconcile the 43.5% (intro) vs 45.8% (experiments) discrepancy?</li>
<li>What is the size of each domain-specific calibration set?</li>
<li>Can you provide the actual graph-structured CP algorithm (currently duplicated as Algorithm 2)?</li>
<li>Was PhyX evaluation multimodal or text-only?</li>
</ul>
